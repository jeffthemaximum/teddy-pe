## Drill glossary, this week

**Speed & Acceleration**

- **A-march.** A slow marching drill that teaches the sprinting leg action one step at a time. Watch for: Leaning back, or the foot reaching out in front of the body on the way down. Cue: "Knee up, foot down under you, stay tall."
- **Build-up runs.** Gradual runs that ramp from easy to nearly fast, used to warm up before anything quick. Watch for: Starting at full speed. These are the ramp, not the race. Cue: "Roll into it like a plane taking off."
- **High knees.** The A-march done at a quick running tempo over a short distance. Watch for: Bending at the waist, or feet slapping the ground. Cue: "Quick feet, tall body."
- **Line-touch.** A shuttle test: sprint 5m, touch, sprint back, touch, sprint out again. Watch for: Rounding the turn instead of stopping and touching. The braking is the point. Cue: "Low on the turns, hand all the way down."
- **Ready position.** The athletic stance applied to a sport, waiting for the ball to tell you where to go. Watch for: Standing upright, or hands dropping down by the hips. Cue: "Hands up, knees soft, nose over toes."
- **Standing start.** Starting a sprint from a still standing position, the format used for the 20m test. Watch for: Standing straight up on the first step, or rocking backward before going forward. Cue: "Fall, then run."

**Power & Landing**

- **Broad jump.** A two-footed jump for distance from a standing start. Watch for: Falling forward or stepping after landing. Measure to the back heel. Cue: "Arms back, then throw them at the horizon."
- **Silent Landings.** The Week 1 challenge: ten jumps off a step, counting how many make no sound at all. Watch for: Quiet comes from bending the knees and landing toes first, then heels. Cue: "Sneak up on the ground."
- **Single-leg hop.** Hopping for distance off one leg and landing on that same leg. Watch for: A gap of more than 15 percent between legs is worth flagging and evening out. Cue: "Same foot takes off, same foot lands."
- **Single-leg stick.** A small hop landed and held on one leg for three seconds. Watch for: The knee falling inward on landing, or arms windmilling for balance. Cue: "Quiet landing, then statue."
- **Stick landing.** Landing from a jump and freezing completely still, quiet and balanced. Watch for: Knees caving inward, a loud landing, or a small hop after touching down. If the knees cave, lower the step. Cue: "Land like a cat."

**Coordination & Balance**

- **Bunny hops.** Hands on a line, both feet kicked over to the other side and landed together. Cartwheel step 1. Watch for: Bent arms, or looking at his feet instead of his hands. Cue: "Arms straight, look at your hands."
- **Cartwheel step 1.** The first rung of the cartwheel ladder: bunny hops over a line, getting used to weight on the hands. Watch for: Arms folding. Weight on the hands is the skill being built. Cue: "Push the floor away."
- **Cartwheel step 2.** Four beats along a line: hand, hand, foot, foot. The cartwheel rhythm without the height. Watch for: Hands landing off the line, or both feet leaving at once. It is four separate beats. Cue: "Hand, hand, foot, foot."
- **Donkey kicks.** Hands on the floor near a wall, kicking the hips and feet up toward it. Handstand preparation. Watch for: Bent arms, or crashing the feet into the wall. Control on the way down matters more than height. Cue: "Push the ground away and float the hips up."
- **Gallops.** Sideways or forward skipping where the same foot always leads, like a horse. Watch for: The back foot crossing past the lead foot, which turns it into running. Cue: "One foot is the boss, the other one chases."
- **Jump rope.** Basic single-unders: one rope pass per jump, counting the streak. Watch for: Big arm circles and big jumps, the two things that end a streak. Cue: "Tiny hops, wrists not arms."
- **Safe fall.** Learning to fall onto the side and forearm instead of onto straight arms. Watch for: Reaching out with a straight arm. That is exactly how wrists break. Cue: "Bend and roll, never catch."
- **Single-leg balance.** Standing on one leg for time, eventually with the eyes closed. Watch for: Gripping hard with the toes, or the standing knee locking straight. Cue: "Soft knee, quiet foot, look at one spot."
- **Skateboard cruise.** Unstructured skateboarding, in the program as balance and confidence work rather than a drill. Watch for: Fatigue. Skate first in the session, not last. Cue: "Knees soft, look where you are going."
- **Skips.** Ordinary playground skipping, used here as rhythm and elastic work. Watch for: Flat heavy landings. Skips should sound light. Cue: "Up, not out."

**Strength & Resilience**

- **Animal walk medley.** A string of animal crawls back to back, the standing warm-up of this program. Watch for: Rushing. Slow and strong beats fast and floppy every time. Cue: "Be the animal, not a kid in a hurry."
- **Arm circles.** Shoulder warm-up, small circles growing into big ones, both directions. Watch for: Shrugging the shoulders up toward the ears. Cue: "Draw circles on the wall behind you."
- **Bear walk.** Crawling on hands and feet with the hips high and the knees off the ground. Watch for: Knees dropping to the floor, or the hips rocking side to side. Cue: "Flat back, like a table you could put a drink on."
- **Crab walk.** Belly up, hands and feet on the ground, hips lifted, walking in any direction. Watch for: The bottom dragging along the floor. That means the hips gave up. Cue: "Hips to the sky."
- **Dead hang.** Hanging from a bar with straight arms for as long as possible. Watch for: Thumbs not wrapped, or swinging to buy time. Cue: "Strong hands, long arms."
- **Hollow hold.** Lying on the back in a shallow banana shape with shoulders and legs off the floor. Watch for: A gap under the lower back. If it lifts, bend the knees or lower the legs. Cue: "Squeeze everything, back glued to the floor."
- **Inchworms.** Bending to the floor, walking the hands out to a plank, then walking the feet up to the hands. Watch for: Hips sagging in the plank position. Cue: "Long body in the middle, no banana back."
- **Traveling rings.** The swinging rings in Riverside Park: swing from one ring to the next down the line. Watch for: Reaching too early, before the swing is carrying him forward. Cue: "Swing first, then reach at the front."

**Throw & Catch**

- **Overhand throw.** A full throw over the top of the shoulder, tested for distance with each arm. Watch for: Throwing from the elbow with a still body. The power comes from the hips turning first. Cue: "Hips first, arm last."
- **Step-and-throw.** The foundation throwing pattern: step with the opposite foot, then throw. Watch for: Stepping with the same-side foot, or throwing with only the arm while the feet stay still. Cue: "Opposite foot steps at the target."

**Tennis**

- **Contact in front.** Meeting the ball out in front of the body rather than beside or behind it. Watch for: Late contact beside the hip. That is the first sign the ball is too fast or the split step was missed. Cue: "Hit it where you can see it."
- **Drop feed.** The simplest feed there is: he drops the ball himself and hits it after the bounce. Watch for: Dropping the ball too close to the body, which forces a cramped swing. Cue: "Drop it where you want to hit it."
- **Finish over the shoulder.** Ending the swing with the racket up over the opposite shoulder, which proves the low-to-high path. Watch for: Stopping the racket at the ball. A short finish usually means he is steering it. Cue: "Catch the racket by your ear."
- **Green ball.** The transition ball at 75 percent of normal pressure, the default rally ball this year. Watch for: Nothing to watch here, this is a reference entry. Cue: "Green is the form ball."
- **Rally count.** A cooperative rally with Dad where the goal is keeping it going, not winning. Watch for: Turning it competitive. Cooperative means both of them are on the same side. Cue: "How long can we keep it alive?"
- **Shadow swings.** Full strokes swung with no ball, so all the attention goes on the shape. Watch for: Rushing them. Slow shadow swings are where the form actually changes. Cue: "Same swing every time, like a stamp."
- **Split step.** A tiny hop that lands just as the opponent hits, so you are light and able to go either way. Watch for: A big jump, or landing flat-footed and stuck. Cue: "Land ready."
- **Unit turn.** Turning the shoulders, hips and racket together as one piece at the start of the stroke. Watch for: The arm going back while the chest stays facing the net. Cue: "Turn like a door, all in one piece."
- **Wall rally.** Rallying against a practice wall, counting how many in a row. Watch for: Standing too close, which turns it into fast blocking instead of real strokes. Cue: "Back up, full swing, count out loud."

**Basketball**

- **Bounce pass.** The same push as a chest pass, aimed at the floor about two thirds of the way to the target. Watch for: Bouncing it too close to himself, which makes the ball arrive too high and slow. Cue: "Bounce it two thirds of the way."
- **Chest pass.** A two-handed pass pushed straight from the chest to a target. Watch for: Winding up behind the head first, which is slow and easy to read. Cue: "Step and push, thumbs down."
- **Cone weave.** Dribbling in and out of a line of cones, tested for time. Watch for: Swinging wide around the cones. Tight turns are the skill. Cue: "Close to the cones, low and quick."
- **Crossover.** Bouncing the ball across the body from one hand to the other. Watch for: Standing up, or the bounce going too wide where a defender could take it. Cue: "Low and quick, right in front of you."
- **Figure 8.** Weaving the ball in a figure of eight around and between both legs. Watch for: Looking down at the ball, which is the whole habit being trained out. Cue: "Eyes on my fingers, not the ball."
- **Jump stop.** Stopping from a dribble by landing on both feet at exactly the same time. Watch for: One foot landing first. Then only the other foot can pivot, and it is a travel if he picks the wrong one. Cue: "Both feet, same moment, stick it."
- **Pivot.** Keeping one foot planted and stepping around it with the other. Watch for: The pivot foot sliding or lifting, which is a travel. Cue: "That foot is glued down."
- **Pound dribble.** Hard stationary dribbling at about knee height, one hand at a time. Watch for: Slapping with a flat palm, or the ball creeping up above the waist. Cue: "Eyes up, ball low, pound it."
- **Triple threat.** The stance holding the ball where shooting, passing or driving are all still possible. Watch for: Standing up straight, or holding the ball out where it can be slapped away. Cue: "Low and ready, make them guess."

**Soccer**

- **Cone gate.** Two cones set a short distance apart making a little goal to pass or dribble through. Watch for: Aiming at a cone instead of at the space between them. Cue: "Through the middle, not at the cones."
- **Inside-foot pass.** The basic accurate pass, struck with the flat inside of the foot. Watch for: A floppy ankle, or the plant foot pointing somewhere other than the target. Cue: "Lock the ankle, point the plant foot where it's going."
- **Scoop.** The goalkeeper's pickup for balls rolling along the ground. Watch for: Bending only at the waist with straight legs, and leaving a gap between the hands and the legs. Cue: "Get behind it, scoop and hug."
- **Sole rolls.** Rolling the ball sideways across the body using the sole of one foot. Watch for: Standing over the ball too upright, or letting the foot slip off the top. Cue: "Squash it and roll it."
- **Tick-tocks.** Tapping the ball side to side between the insides of both feet, like a metronome. Watch for: The ball drifting further away with each tap, or his head dropping to watch it. Cue: "Tick, tock, like a clock."
- **Toe taps.** Tapping the top of a still ball with the sole of alternating feet, quick and light. Watch for: Standing flat-footed, or the ball rolling away because the taps are too heavy. Cue: "Light and fast, like the ball is hot."
- **W-catch.** The goalkeeper's catch for balls at chest height and above, thumbs together behind the ball. Watch for: Thumbs apart, which lets the ball through between the hands. Cue: "Hands first, then hug it."
- **Wall passes.** Passing against a wall and controlling the rebound, over and over. Watch for: Letting the ball get too far away, or using only the strong foot. Cue: "Pass, catch it with your foot, pass again."

**Compete & Mindset**

- **Between-point reset.** A three-part routine after every point that clears the last one and starts the next. Watch for: Skipping it when he is annoyed. That is the exact moment it is worth the most. Cue: "Breathe, bounce, strings."
- **Champion's Log.** Teddy's own notebook, one line a day, written by him. Watch for: Turning it into homework. One line is the whole ask. Cue: "One line. Yours, not mine."
- **Dribble tag.** A tag game where everyone keeps dribbling, with Dad stationary this fall. Watch for: Head dropping when it gets hard. That is the thing the game is teaching. Cue: "Eyes up even when you are being chased."
- **HORSE.** The shooting game: match the other player's made shot or take a letter. Watch for: Nothing technical. This is a competing game, and losing well is part of the point. Cue: "Same shot, your turn."
- **Shake out.** The five-minute close to every session: walk, stretch the calves, breathe, write the log line. Watch for: Skipping it on good days. It is the habit that protects a tall frame later. Cue: "Walk it off, breathe, write your line."
- **Sharks and minnows.** A crossing game where the shark tries to tag runners, played this fall with Dad stationary. Watch for: Nothing technical. It is change of direction disguised as a game. Cue: "Wait for your moment, then go."
